    Sources and limits

    Bones and brain parts are real 3D data. They come from BodyParts3D, © The Database Center for Life Science, licensed under CC Attribution 4.0 International, an adult male reference body built from MRI. Geometry was simplified for the browser by the open source Human Atlas project.

    Nerve routes are schematic. BodyParts3D has almost no peripheral nerves, so each nerve here is a curve drawn along the real bones following standard textbook anatomy: which roots it comes from, which side of which bone it runs on, where it branches. Positions are accurate to roughly a centimetre or two, good for learning routes, not for clinical use.

    • Nerves included: the spinal cord, all 31 pairs of spinal nerves, all 12 pairs of cranial nerves, and the main named nerves of the cervical, brachial, lumbar and sacral plexuses, the intercostal nerves and the sympathetic trunks. Small unnamed twigs are not drawn.
    • The brain is mapped by region and by major pathway. A neuron by neuron map is not possible: the human brain has about 86 billion neurons and no complete wiring diagram of it exists. Full maps exist only for tiny animals, such as a roundworm with 302 neurons and a fruit fly with about 140,000.
    • Travel times use typical conduction speeds for each fibre type (about 100 m/s for the fastest reflex fibres, about 1 m/s for slow pain fibres) plus a short delay at each synapse. Real values vary between people and with temperature.

    A good free textbook for checking and going deeper is OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology. Gray's Anatomy (1918 edition) is also in the public domain.

    Educational model, not a diagnostic tool.
